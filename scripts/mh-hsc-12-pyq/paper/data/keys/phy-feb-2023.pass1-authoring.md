## Working

**Q. 1(i) — C.** A molecule sweeps a collision cylinder of cross-section
\(\pi d^2\); correcting for the relative motion of the other molecules
contributes the \(\sqrt2\). Hence
\(\lambda = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi n d^{2}}\). Option (d) has \(d\) instead of
\(d^2\) (dimensionally wrong), (b) drops the \(\sqrt2\), (a) is not even a
length.

**Q. 1(ii) — B.** \(\Delta U = Q - W\) is the statement that energy is conserved
once heat is counted as a form of energy transfer. Momentum and mass
conservation are separate principles; velocity is not conserved at all.

**Q. 1(iii) — C.** \(A + B\) is OR and the overbar is NOT, so \(\overline{A+B}\)
is NOR. NAND would be \(\overline{A \cdot B}\).

**Q. 1(iv) — D.** Frequency is set by the source and cannot change at a boundary
(the fields must stay in step across it). Speed changes as \(v = c/\mu\) and
wavelength follows \(\lambda = v/\nu\). Amplitude changes too, through
reflection and transmission losses.

**Q. 1(v) — C.** "Plane perpendicular to the field" means the field is along the
normal, so \(\theta = 0\) and \(\cos\theta = 1\). Flux linkage
\(= NBA = 100 \times 1 \times 1 = 100\) Wb. Option (a) is the flux through a
single turn — the trap is forgetting \(N\).

**Q. 1(vi) — A.** The angle of contact measures how strongly adhesion beats
cohesion. Complete wetting is the limiting case where the liquid spreads
entirely, \(\theta = 0\). Acute \(\theta\) is partial wetting; obtuse is
non-wetting (mercury on glass).

**Q. 1(vii) — C.** Forward bias injects carriers across the junction; each
recombination releases the band-gap energy as a photon. Reverse bias widens the
depletion layer and gives no emission.

**Q. 1(viii) — A.** A transformer core is re-magnetised 50 times a second in
each direction, so it must give up its magnetism easily and waste as little
energy per cycle as possible. That needs a **narrow** hysteresis loop — low
coercivity **and** low retentivity — which is exactly what soft iron provides.
High-retentivity, high-coercivity materials (steel, alnico) are for permanent
magnets, where you want the opposite.

**Q. 1(ix) — D.** The stopping potential is defined by \(eV_0 = K_{\max}\). With
\(K_{\max} = 2\) eV, \(V_0 = 2.0\) V numerically. The electron-volt is built so
this is a direct read-off.

**Q. 1(x) — B.** Bohr radii go as \(r_n = a_0 n^2 / Z\). For hydrogen,
\(r_8/r_4 = 8^2/4^2 = 64/16 = 4\). Option (a) 2 is the \(n\)-ratio, not the
radius ratio; (d) 16 would be \((n_8/n_4)^4\).

## Flags

- **Provenance caveat applies to every row.** This is a commercial reproduction:
  its option labels are `(A)`–`(D)` where every board print in this lane uses
  `(a)`–`(d)`, and it numbers Section D's two-part items `Q.28(i)/(ii)` rather
  than repeating a bare number. Those are re-typesetting choices, not board
  ones. A disagreement between this paper and the bank is therefore a flag for a
  human, never a verdict — the bank's rows came from the same sitting but a
  different transcription of it.
- **Q. 1(viii) is the one worth a second look at reconciliation.** "Low
  retentivity" is stated in some treatments as "high permeability / high
  saturation", which a reader can mis-map onto "high retentivity". The
  Balbharati soft-vs-hard magnetic materials table lists soft iron as low on
  both, which is what (A) says.
