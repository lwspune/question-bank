## Working

**Q. 1(i) — B.** A uniform disc about the axis through its centre and
perpendicular to its plane has \(I = \tfrac12 MR^2\). \(MR^2\) is the *ring*
about the same axis (offered as (c)), \(MR^2/4\) is the disc about a diameter,
and \(\tfrac32 MR^2\) is a ring about a tangent in its plane.

**Q. 1(ii) — D.** Surface tension is force per unit length:
\([T] = \dfrac{MLT^{-2}}{L} = M^1 T^{-2}\), so the length exponent is zero —
\([L^0M^1T^{-2}]\). Option (a) is pressure, (b) is force × length.

**Q. 1(iii) — B.** A node and its adjacent antinode are \(\lambda/4\) apart, and
\(k\Delta x = \dfrac{2\pi}{\lambda}\cdot\dfrac{\lambda}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{2}\).
*See the flag: read as a temporal phase this is arguably 0, not \(\pi/2\).*

**Q. 1(iv) — C.** Electric potential at a point is defined as the work done per
unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to that point against the
field. Flux is \(\oint \vec E \cdot d\vec A\); the other two are different
fields entirely.

**Q. 1(v) — A.** An ammeter carries the line current, so it must present almost
no resistance: a small shunt in parallel diverts most of the current past the
coil. A large series resistance is the *voltmeter* conversion, offered as (b).

**Q. 1(vi) — C.** \(K_{\max} = h\nu - \phi\). Doubling the frequency gives
\(K' = 2h\nu - \phi = 2(h\nu - \phi) + \phi = 2K_{\max} + \phi\). Since
\(\phi > 0\), the new maximum kinetic energy is strictly **more** than twice the
old — the work function is subtracted once, not twice. (b) is the trap for
treating \(K\) as proportional to \(\nu\).

**Q. 1(vii) — D.** Internal energy is a state function, so over a closed cycle
\(\Delta U = 0\). The first law \(Q = \Delta U + W\) then gives \(Q = W\).
Options (b) and (c) are the adiabatic and isochoric cases respectively.

**Q. 1(viii) — D.** \(|e| = L\left|\dfrac{dI}{dt}\right| =
20 \times \dfrac{50 - 10}{0.1} = 20 \times 400 = 8000\) V.

**Q. 1(ix) — A.** A second's pendulum has \(T = 2\) s, so
\(\omega = 2\pi/T = \pi\) rad/s. Then
\(v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} = \pi\sqrt{10^2 - 6^2} = \pi\sqrt{64} = 8\pi\)
cm/s. The 6–8–10 triple is the giveaway that this is the intended route.

**Q. 1(x) — D.** In a biprism pattern the distance of the \(n\)th bright band
from the centre is \(n\beta\), linear in \(n\) with the set-up unchanged. So
\(x_{30} = x_{20}\times\dfrac{30}{20} = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\) cm.

## Flags

- **Q. 1(iii) depends on which phase is meant, and the options force one
  reading.** In a stationary wave \(y = 2A\sin kx \cos\omega t\), every particle
  within a loop oscillates in step, so the *temporal* phase difference between a
  node and its neighbouring antinode is **zero**. The \(\pi/2\) comes from the
  *spatial* phase \(k\Delta x\) over the \(\lambda/4\) separation. Zero is not on
  the option list, so the spatial reading is the intended one and the answer is
  \(\pi/2\). Recorded at medium confidence because the stem does not say which.
