## Working

**Q. 1(i) — D.** The perpendicular-axes theorem \(I_z = I_x + I_y\) is derived
from \(z=0\) for every mass element, which is only true of a planar (laminar)
body. Spheres, cones and cylinders are three-dimensional.

**Q. 1(ii) — B.** A wave \(\sin(kx - \omega t)\) keeps phase constant when \(x\)
increases with \(t\), i.e. it moves toward \(+x\). The sign flip
\(\sin(kx + \omega t)\) therefore travels toward \(-x\). Options (c) and (d) use
\(ky\), which is not motion along \(x\) at all.

**Q. 1(iii) — B.** At exit \(qvB = mv^2/R\) gives \(v = qBR/m\), so
\(\text{K.E.} = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \dfrac{q^2B^2R^2}{2m} \propto R_{\text{exit}}^2\).

**Q. 1(iv) — D.** Visible photons run ~1.8–3.1 eV. Zinc (~4.3 eV), cadmium
(~4.1 eV) and magnesium (~3.7 eV) all exceed that; sodium at ~2.3 eV does not.

**Q. 1(v) — C.** For an orbiting electron the magnetic moment is
\(\mu_L = \tfrac{evr}{2}\) and the angular momentum \(L = m_e vr\), so
\(\dfrac{\mu_L}{L} = \dfrac{e}{2m_e}\). The distractors invert it or square the
charge. *This is the question whose options (c) and (d) the text layer renders
identically.*

**Q. 1(vi) — B.** \(h = \dfrac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g} \propto \dfrac1r\). With
\(r_1 = 0.3\) and \(r_2 = 0.6\), \(h_1:h_2 = 2:1\).

**Q. 1(vii) — C.** Kirchhoff's voltage law says the algebraic sum of potential
differences round a closed loop is zero — a unit charge returning to its start
has done zero net work. The junction law is the charge-conservation one.

**Q. 1(viii) — A.** Unpolarised light is an even mixture of all transverse
orientations; averaging \(\cos^2\theta\) over a full turn gives \(\tfrac12\).

**Q. 1(ix) — B.** Constant volume gives \(P \propto T\), so
\(\dfrac{\Delta P}{P} = \dfrac{\Delta T}{T}\). With \(\Delta P/P = 0.004\) and
\(\Delta T = 1\), \(T = 250\) **kelvin**. The options are printed in °C, and
250 °C = 523 K would give 0.19%, not 0.4%. Recorded as B because 250 is the only
numeral the arithmetic can produce and all four options carry the same wrong
unit — see the flag.

**Q. 1(x) — A.** \(E = hc/\lambda = (6.63\times10^{-34})(3\times10^{8})/
(4\times10^{-7}) = 4.9725\times10^{-19}\) J. Then
\(n = P/E = 0.1/4.9725\times10^{-19} = 2.011\times10^{17}\).
