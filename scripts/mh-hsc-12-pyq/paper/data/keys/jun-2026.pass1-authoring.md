## Working

**(iii) THE TRAP ON THIS PAPER, and both candidate answers are options.**

Printed: `p·(q×r) + q·(p×r) + r·(p×q)`. The middle term is **anti-cyclic** — the
standard identity cycles the letters as `q·(r×p)`. With `p=i, q=j, r=k`:

| term | value |
|---|---|
| `i·(j×k) = i·i` | `+1` |
| `j·(i×k) = j·(-j)` | `-1` |
| `k·(i×j) = k·k` | `+1` |

**As printed the sum is 1, option (c).** The cyclic form gives 3, option (d), and
**3 is also on the list**. Solved as printed, per the brief — a repaired stem here
produces a different letter and looks perfectly sound doing it. See
`data/ADJUDICATIONS.md`.

**(iv)** Direction ratios `B - A = (2-3, -1-4, 3-(-1)) = (-1, -5, 4)`, through

the sign of the `y` denominator, which is the natural slip.

**(v)** `y = log_x a = ln a / ln x`. Then
`dy/dx = ln a · d/dx (ln x)^{-1} = -ln a / (x (ln x)^2)`. sympy agrees. Option A.

**(vi)** With `u = sin^{-1} x`, `du = dx/sqrt(1-x^2)`, the integral is
`∫ u e^u du = e^u (u - 1) + c`. sympy returns `(asin(x) - 1)*exp(asin(x))`, and
differentiating option B's expression minus the integrand simplifies to exactly
`0`. Option B. Option C has the sign of the bracket reversed.

**(vii)** `y = 2x` is NEGATIVE on `(-1, 0)`, so the bounded AREA is not the
signed integral. `∫_{-1}^{4} 2x dx = 15` — which is option (a), the trap. The
area is `∫_{-1}^{4} |2x| dx = 1 + 16 = 17`. "Area bounded by" means area.
Option B.

**(viii)** `E(X) = np = 6` and `Var(X) = npq = 4.2`, so `q = 4.2/6 = 0.7`,

`npq = 20(0.3)(0.7) = 4.2`, both as given. Option C.
