## Working

**Q. 1(i) — C.** "Central axis" of a ring is the axis through its centre
perpendicular to its plane. Every mass element sits at the same distance \(R\)
from it, so \(I=\sum m_i R^2 = MR^2\). The distractors are the standard
confusions: \(MR^2/2\) is a *disc* about the same axis, and \(\tfrac32 MR^2\) is
a ring about a tangent in its plane... no, that is \(\tfrac32 MR^2\) for a ring
about a tangent in its own plane, which is exactly why it is offered.

**Q. 1(ii) — B.** A monoatomic molecule is a point mass: 3 translational degrees
of freedom, no rotational or vibrational contribution at ordinary temperature.
\(f=3\). (5 is diatomic, 7 is a diatomic with vibration.)

**Q. 1(iii) — A.** First law: \(Q=\Delta U + W\). Isochoric means \(\Delta V=0\),
so \(W=\int p\,dV=0\), leaving \(Q=\Delta U\). Option (b) \(Q=0\) is the
*adiabatic* case, (c) is the general statement, (d) is the general statement with
a sign error. Note the paper prints \(Q\) where Balbharati prints \(\Delta Q\);
the content is the same and (a) is the only reduction offered.

**Q. 1(iv) — C.** A cyclotron works by the magnetic force \(qvB\) on a charge
plus a reversing electric field across the dees. A neutron has \(q=0\), so it
experiences neither. Protons, \(\alpha\)-particles and deuterons are all charged.

**Q. 1(v) — B.** Power factor is \(\cos\phi\). In a purely inductive circuit
current lags by \(90^\circ\); in a purely capacitive one it leads by \(90^\circ\).
Either way \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\), and the average power is zero — the circuit is
wattless. Option (d) "10" is not a possible value of a cosine at all.

**Q. 1(vi) — D.** NOR = NOT(OR), so \(Y=\overline{A+B}\). Option (a)
\(Y=A\cdot B\) is AND, (b) \(Y=A+B\) is OR, (c) \(Y=\overline{A}\) is NOT.
Note (a), (b) and (d) are the three options the text layer renders identically —
this key is only meaningful because the options were read off the page.

**Q. 1(vii) — C.** Capacitors in series add reciprocally:
\(\dfrac{1}{C_s}=\dfrac{1}{C}+\dfrac{1}{C}+\cdots\) (\(n\) terms) \(=\dfrac{n}{C}\),
so \(C_s=\dfrac{C}{n}\). Option (d) \(1/(nC)\) is the reciprocal of the series
capacitance, i.e. dimensionally wrong.

**Q. 1(viii) — B.** Brewster's law: \(\mu=\tan\theta_p\).
\(\tan 35^\circ = 0.70021\), which is option (b) to four places.

*Solved as printed.* The physics of the printed question is unsound: a
refractive index must exceed 1 for light entering a denser medium, which needs
\(\theta_p>45^\circ\). At \(35^\circ\) every arithmetically reachable answer is
below 1, and all four printed options are below 1 — so the board intends
\(\tan 35^\circ\) and has simply chosen an impossible angle. The computation is
unambiguous even though the situation is not physical. Flagged rather than
repaired.

**Q. 1(ix) — D.** Velocity gradient is \(\dfrac{dv}{dx}\), here
\(\dfrac{10\ \text{cm/s}}{5\ \text{cm}} = 2\) per second. The centimetres cancel,
so no conversion is needed. Printed unit is "S\(^{-1}\)" with a capital S on all
four options; capital S is the siemens, so this is a printed typo for s\(^{-1}\),
but it is shared by every option and therefore inert.

**Q. 1(x) — A.** \(v=n\lambda\) where \(n\) is the frequency:
\(v = 64 \times 0.75 = 48\) m/s. The distractors are the two given numbers
themselves (64) and round values near the answer.

## Flags

- **Q. 1(viii): the printed question is not physically realisable.** A polarising
  angle of \(35^\circ\) implies \(\mu=0.70<1\). Every option is below 1, so the
  board built the whole item on the impossible value; the intended computation
  (\(\tan 35^\circ\)) is still unambiguous. Preserved as printed.
- **Q. 1(ix): "S\(^{-1}\)" should be "s\(^{-1}\)"** — capital S is siemens.
  Printed on all four options, so it cannot affect the choice. Preserved.
- **Q. 1(v): option (d) is "10"**, which is not a possible power factor. A weak
  distractor, not a defect.
