## Working

**Q. 1(i) — A.** The zeroth law is exactly the transitivity of thermal
equilibrium, and it is what makes temperature definable. The first law is energy
conservation, the second concerns entropy, and "Carnot's law" is not a law of
thermodynamics.

**Q. 1(ii) — D.** \(P + \tfrac12\rho v^2 + \rho gh = \text{constant}\) — each
term is an energy per unit volume, so the conserved quantity is energy.

**Q. 1(iii) — C.** Of wood, iron, glass and copper, only glass transmits
radiation at all; the metals and wood are opaque. *See the flag — the textbook's
own greenhouse treatment complicates this.*

**Q. 1(iv) — C.** \(V = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q}{r}\), so
\(V \propto 1/r\). (The **field** goes as \(1/r^2\); that is the trap.)

**Q. 1(v) — D.** With the cell on open circuit the balancing length gives \(E\)
and with a resistance \(R\) across it the balancing length gives \(V\). From
\(E = I(R+r)\) and \(V = IR\): \(\dfrac{E}{V} = \dfrac{R+r}{R}\), so
\(r = R\left(\dfrac{E}{V} - 1\right)\). Since \(E > V\) always, this is positive,
which rules out (b) and (c); (a) inverts the ratio.

**Q. 1(vi) — B.** \(\lambda = h/\sqrt{2mK}\), longest for the smallest mass, so
the electron. **On this paper the electron is option (b)** — see the flag.

**Q. 1(vii) — B.** AND is high only when both inputs are high. X-OR is high when
the inputs *differ*; NAND and NOR are both low for two high inputs.

**Q. 1(viii) — D.** \(P = \tau\omega = 2\ \text{Nm} \times 2\pi\ \text{rad/s}
= 4\pi\) W.

**Q. 1(ix) — A.** Two loops across a 2 m string means each loop is 1 m, and a
loop is half a wavelength, so \(\lambda = 2\) m. The node-to-adjacent-antinode
distance is a quarter wavelength: \(\lambda/4 = 0.5\) m.

**Q. 1(x) — D.** \(\dfrac{N_s}{N_p} = \dfrac{E_s}{E_p} = \dfrac{880}{220} = 4\),
so \(N_s = 4000\).
