=== 2
subject: Mathematics
chapter: Quadrilaterals
subtopic: Trapeziums
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
note: No trapezium has parallel sides 60 cm and 18 cm with legs 65 cm and 10 cm (the legs' horizontal parts would have to differ by more than 42 cm), so the printed figure cannot exist; the intended answer from the midpoint rule is still 21 cm.
stem:
ABCD is a quadrilateral in which AB || DC, and E and F are the mid points of the diagonals AC and BD, respectively. If AB = 60 cm, BC = 65 cm, DC = 18 cm, and AD = 10 cm, then what is the length (in cm) of EF?
A: 23
B: 21
C: 25
D: 22
solution:
In a trapezium, the segment joining the midpoints of the diagonals is parallel to the bases and equals half their difference. BC and AD are not needed.

\(EF = \frac{AB - DC}{2} = \frac{60 - 18}{2} = 21\) cm.

Check: the midpoint of AD to E is \(\frac{DC}{2} = 9\) and the midpoint of AD to F is \(\frac{AB}{2} = 30\) along the midline, so \(EF = 30 - 9 = 21\).

Note: with these side lengths no such trapezium can actually be drawn (legs of 65 cm and 10 cm cannot meet bases 42 cm apart in length), but the answer the question intends is 21 cm.

stem:
Let both a and b be prime numbers such that \((a^2 + b)\) is also a prime number less than 20. What is the number of possible values of b?
A: 6
B: 3
C: 5
D: 4
solution:
\(a^2 + b\) is an odd prime (it is more than 2), so exactly one of \(a\) and \(b\) is 2.

If \(a = 2\): \(4 + b\) must be a prime below 20. \(b = 3\) gives 7, \(b = 7\) gives 11, \(b = 13\) gives 17 (\(b = 5, 11, 17\) give 9, 15, 21, not prime).

If \(b = 2\): \(a^2 + 2\) with \(a = 3\) gives 11 (\(a = 5\) gives 27).

So \(b\) can be 2, 3, 7 or 13: 4 values.
