subject: Mathematics
chapter: Circles
subtopic: Angles in a Circle
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Let C be a circle with centre O. Let AB be a tangent to C such that AB touches the circle at point P. Let X and Y be any two points on C. If \(\angle\)APX = \(50^\circ\) and \(\angle\)BPY = \(60^\circ\), then find \(\angle\)PXY and \(\angle\)PYX, respectively.
A: \(60^\circ\) , \(50^\circ\)
B: \(50^\circ\) , \(60^\circ\)
C: \(50^\circ\) , \(130^\circ\)
D: \(60^\circ\), \(120^\circ\)
solution:
By the alternate segment theorem, the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle the chord makes in the alternate segment.

Chord PX with tangent PA: \(\angle\)APX \(= \angle\)PYX \(= 50^\circ\).

Chord PY with tangent PB: \(\angle\)BPY \(= \angle\)PXY \(= 60^\circ\).

So \(\angle\)PXY \(= 60^\circ\) and \(\angle\)PYX \(= 50^\circ\).

Check: in triangle PXY, \(\angle\)XPY \(= 180^\circ - 50^\circ - 60^\circ = 70^\circ\), and the three angles at P along the line AB add up to \(50^\circ + 70^\circ + 60^\circ = 180^\circ\).

subject: Mathematics
chapter: Time and Work
subtopic: Pipes and Cisterns
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
A tank is filled by three pipes, P, Q and R, with uniform flow. The first two pipes P and Q, operating simultaneously, fill the tank in the same amount of time that it takes for the tank to be filled by pipe R alone. Pipe Q fills the tank 15 hours faster than the first pipe and 12 hours slower than pipe R. What is the time required by pipe P to fill the tank?
A: 32 hours
B: 45 hours
C: 25 hours
D: 47 hours
solution:
Let Q take \(q\) hours. Then P takes \(q + 15\) hours and R takes \(q - 12\) hours.

\(\frac{1}{q + 15} + \frac{1}{q} = \frac{1}{q - 12}\)

Cross-multiplying: \((2q + 15)(q - 12) = q(q + 15)\), so \(2q^2 - 9q - 180 = q^2 + 15q\), giving \(q^2 - 24q - 180 = 0\).

\((q - 30)(q + 6) = 0\), so \(q = 30\) and P takes \(30 + 15 = 45\) hours.

Check: \(\frac{1}{45} + \frac{1}{30} = \frac{2 + 3}{90} = \frac{1}{18}\), and R takes \(30 - 12 = 18\) hours.

subject: Reasoning
chapter: Order, Ranking and Arrangement
subtopic: Linear and Circular Seating
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
D, E, F, G, S, T and U are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. T sits to the immediate left of S. U sits to the immediate left of D. D sits second to the left of G. E sits third to the left of S. Who sits fourth to the right of F?
A: S
B: G
C: D
D: T
solution:
Facing the centre, moving left is clockwise. Number the seats 1 to 7 clockwise and put S at 1.

T is immediately left of S, so T is at 2. E is third to the left of S, so E is at 4.

Clockwise from S: S, T, G, E, D, U, F.

Fourth to the right of F (anticlockwise from 7): 6, 5, 4, 3, so G.
