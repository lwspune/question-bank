subtopic: Pipes and Cisterns
difficulty: MODERATE

stem:
Pipe A can fill a cistern in 12 hours and another pipe B can empty it in 18 hours. Pipe A is opened first and after 4 hours, pipe B is also opened. In how much total time will the cistern be filled?
A: 36 hours
B: 32 hours
C: 28 hours
D: 24 hours
solution:
In 4 hours A fills \(\frac{4}{12} = \frac{1}{3}\) of the cistern, leaving \(\frac{2}{3}\).

Together the net rate is \(\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{18} = \frac{3 - 2}{36} = \frac{1}{36}\) per hour, so \(\frac{2}{3}\) takes \(\frac{2}{3} \times 36 = 24\) hours.

Total time \(= 4 + 24 = 28\) hours.

Check with a capacity of 36 units: A fills 3 units an hour, B empties 2. In 4 hours A fills 12 units; the other 24 units at 1 unit an hour take 24 hours.

chapter: Matter and Its States
subtopic: States of Matter, Phase Changes and Diffusion
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
Why do noble gases exist as gases at room temperature, while halogens exist as solids, liquids, or gases down the group?
A: Strong chemical bonds in noble gases
B: Larger atomic size of noble gases
C: Weak intermolecular forces in noble gases
D: High reactivity of noble gases
solution:
Noble gases are monatomic with complete octets, and the only forces between their atoms are very weak van der Waals (dispersion) forces, so they stay gases at room temperature. Halogens form diatomic molecules whose dispersion forces grow strongly down the group (F and Cl gases, Br liquid, I solid). Noble gases form no bonds and are unreactive, so A and D are wrong.
