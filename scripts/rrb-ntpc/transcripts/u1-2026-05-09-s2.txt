difficulty: MODERATE
answer: D
stem:
Two trains, X and Y, start at the same time from Delhi and Mumbai towards each other. After meeting, train X takes 6 hours to reach Mumbai, and train Y takes 8 hours to reach Delhi. If train X’s speed is 60 km/h, what is the speed of train Y?
A: \(28\sqrt{3}\) km/h
B: \(30\sqrt{2}\) km/h
C: \(28\sqrt{2}\) km/h
D: \(30\sqrt{3}\) km/h
solution:
For two bodies starting together towards each other, \(\frac{S_X}{S_Y} = \sqrt{\frac{t_Y}{t_X}}\), where \(t\) is the time each takes after meeting.

\(\frac{60}{S_Y} = \sqrt{\frac{8}{6}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\), so \(S_Y = 30\sqrt{3}\) km/h.

Check: let them meet after \(t\) hours. X covers Y's pre-meeting distance: \(60 \times 6 = S_Y t\), and Y covers X's: \(S_Y \times 8 = 60t\). Dividing, \(\frac{360}{8 S_Y} = \frac{S_Y}{60}\), so \(S_Y^2 = 2700\) and \(S_Y = 30\sqrt{3}\).

difficulty: MODERATE

stem:
If p is a prime number and GCD \((p^2 - 1, p^2 + 1) = 2\), then the value of p must be:
A: Any prime number
B: Any odd prime
C: Only 3
D: Only 2
solution:
Any common divisor of \(p^2 - 1\) and \(p^2 + 1\) divides their difference, 2, so the GCD is 1 or 2.

If p is odd, \(p^2\) is odd, so both numbers are even and the GCD is 2.

If \(p = 2\), the numbers are 3 and 5, whose GCD is 1.

So p can be any odd prime (for example \(p = 5\): GCD(24, 26) = 2, so "only 3" is too narrow).
