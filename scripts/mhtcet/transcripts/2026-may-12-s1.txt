difficulty: HARD
answer: A
stem:
Unpolarized light with intensity \(I_0\) passes through three polaroids such that the transmission axis of the last polaroid is crossed with the first. If the intensity of emerging light is \(I_1\left(I_1 = \dfrac{3}{32}I_0\right)\). The angle between the first two polaroids and the angle for which the transmitted intensity is maximum are respectively \(\{\)given, \(\cos(90) = 0\}\)
A: \(\dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(45^\circ\)
B: \(\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(90^\circ\)
C: \(\dfrac{1}{2}\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(45^\circ\)
D: \(\cos^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(90^\circ\)
solution:
\(I_1 = \dfrac{I_0}{2}\cos^2\theta\sin^2\theta = \dfrac{I_0}{8}\sin^2 2\theta = \dfrac{3}{32}I_0\), so \(\sin 2\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\) and \(\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}\sin^{-1}\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\). The intensity is greatest when \(\sin 2\theta = 1\), at \(45^\circ\).

In Young's double slit experiment, the fringe width is \(2.4\) mm with light of wavelength \(9000\) A.U. If the light of wavelength \(5400\) A.U. is used, with no other change in the experimental set up. The change in fringe width is
A: \(0.86\) mm
B: \(0.96\) mm

D: \(0.96\) cm

A photon of energy \(10\) units ejects a photo-electron from a metal surface whose work function is \(\Phi_0\). difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
In transition 'a', three \(\alpha\) particles and two \(\beta\) particles are emitted in succession from the element \({}_{86}X^{226}\). In transition 'b', two gamma rays, one \(\beta\) particle and two \(\alpha\) particles are emitted in succession from element \({}_{96}Y^{236}\). The atomic number and the mass number of the new elements formed respectively are
A: a) for first transition:- atomic number \(= 83\), mass number \(= 216\) b) for the second transition:- atomic number \(= 82\), mass number \(= 214\)
B: a) for first transition:- atomic number \(= 80\), mass number \(= 225\) b) for the second transition:- atomic number \(= 90\), mass number \(= 220\)
C: a) for first transition:- atomic number \(= 82\), mass number \(= 214\) b) for the second transition:- atomic number \(= 93\), mass number \(= 228\)
D: a) for first transition:- atomic number \(= 80\), mass number \(= 214\) b) for the second transition:- atomic number \(= 90\), mass number \(= 226\)
solution:
(a) \(3\alpha\) lowers the mass number by 12 and the atomic number by 6; \(2\beta\) raises the atomic number by 2: \(Z = 82\), \(A = 214\). (b) Gamma rays change nothing; one \(\beta\) adds 1 to Z and \(2\alpha\) remove 4 from Z and 8 from A: \(Z = 93\), \(A = 228\).

The truth table for the system of four NAND gates as shown in the figure is
A: \((A, B) \to Y\): \(00 \to 1,\ 01 \to 1,\ 10 \to 0,\ 11 \to 0\)
B: \((A, B) \to Y\): \(00 \to 1,\ 01 \to 0,\ 10 \to 0,\ 11 \to 1\)

In one branch, \(40\,\Omega\) resistance and a p-n junction diode are connected in series and in another branch, \(40\,\Omega\) resistance is connected. Both the branches are connected in parallel to each other. The total resistance of the circuit when a) diode is forward biased and when b) diode is reverse biased respectively are {consider the p-n junction diode as an ideal diode}
