=== 2
chapter: Inequalities
subtopic: Direct Inequalities
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: D
stem:
A statement is given followed by two conclusions. Find which conclusion is TRUE based on the given statement.

Statement: \(C \ge T;\ A = D \ge O;\ G \ge D < S;\ T \ge A\)

Conclusions:
I: \(A \le O\)
II: \(C \ge G\)
A: Either I or II
B: Only II
C: Only I
D: Neither I nor II
solution:
From \(A = D \ge O\) we get \(A \ge O\), so \(A \le O\) is not definite: I does not follow.

\(C \ge T \ge A = D\) and \(G \ge D\) put both C and G above D, with no relation between them: II does not follow.

The two conclusions are not a complementary pair (I compares A with O, II compares C with G), so "Either I or II" does not apply. Neither follows.

Read the following statement carefully and identify the conclusion that follows.

A: Only I
B: Only II
C: Both follow
D: Neither follows

subtopic: Statement and Assumptions
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
blind: C
note: judgement item. SSC's key takes I and II as implicit but not III; our blind reading also counted III. The bank carries SSC's answer
stem:
**Instruction:** Identify the assumptions that must hold for the statement to be valid, then choose the correct option.
**Statement:** To reduce traffic congestion in urban areas, cities should invest in public transportation infrastructure, even though it requires significant initial investments.
**Assumptions:**

II. The initial cost of building transportation infrastructure is high.
III. People are willing to shift from private vehicles to public transportation.

B: Only II and III are implicit
C: All I, II and III are implicit
D: Only I is implicit
solution:

II must hold too: the statement itself concedes that the investment is significant, and recommends it anyway.

III goes further than the statement needs. It argues for investment as a way to reduce congestion; whether people will actually switch from private vehicles is a separate question the statement does not take for granted. Only I and II are implicit (SSC's answer).

If \(8 * 2 = 20\) and \(6 * 3 = 27\), then what is \(5 * 4 = ?\)

B: 24
C: 36
D: 22
solution:

subtopic: Word-Code Sentences
difficulty: EASY
answer: A
stem:
**Instruction:** In a code language, "The tree grows tall" is coded as "so mo no lo", "The tree grows short" is coded as "so mo po lo", "The plant grows tall" is coded as "to mo no lo".
**Question:** What is the code for "The plant grows short"?
A: to mo lo po
B: so lo to fo
C: po ko go jo
D: no ko mo no
solution:
The first two sentences differ only in tall/short and in no/po, so tall = no and short = po. The first and third differ only in tree/plant and so/to, so tree = so and plant = to. "The" and "grows" are mo and lo.

"The plant grows short" is therefore to, mo, lo, po in some order: to mo lo po.

subtopic: Number Series
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
The series given below contains a sequence of numbers. Accordingly identify the incorrect combination 4, 5, 14, 14, 34, 36, 74, 68
A: 74
B: 34
C: 36
D: 68
solution:
The series is two series interleaved, each following "double and add".

Odd places, 4, 14, 34, 74: each term is \(2 \times\) the previous \(+ 6\) (\(4 \to 14 \to 34 \to 74\)), which holds throughout.

Even places, 5, 14, 36, 68: the rule is \(2 \times\) the previous \(+ 4\). \(5 \to 14\) holds, and \(14 \to 32\), then \(32 \to 68\). So 36 should be 32 and is the incorrect term.
