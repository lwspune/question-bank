=== 1
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Read the given statements and conclusions carefully. Assuming that the information given in the statements is true, even if it appears to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the given conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
Statements: Some boxes are cartons. All cartons are papers. Some cartons are utensils.
Conclusion (I): No box is a utensil.
Conclusion (II): Some papers are utensils.
A: Only conclusion (II) follows
B: Only conclusion (I) follows
C: Both conclusions (I) and (II) follow
D: Neither conclusion (I) nor (II) follows
solution:
Some cartons are utensils, and every carton is a paper, so those cartons are papers that are utensils: some papers are utensils, and II follows.

Nothing links boxes to utensils directly. A box may or may not be a utensil, so "No box is a utensil" is not definite: I does not follow.

Only conclusion II follows.

subtopic: Symbol Grids
difficulty: HARD
answer: D
figure: stem+options
stem:
Which figure should replace the question mark (?) if the following series were to be continued?
A: [figure]
B: [figure]
C: [figure]
D: [figure]
solution:
Each box holds six symbols in six places: top left, middle centre, middle right, bottom left, bottom centre and bottom right. The boxes read (in that order of places):

Box 1: K, L, 8, X, W, 2. Box 2: X, 2, L, K, 8, W. Box 3: W, L, 2, T, X, 8. Box 4: T, 8, L, W, 2, X.

Steps 1 to 2 and 3 to 4 follow the same rule: the top left and bottom left symbols swap, and the other four move one place along middle centre, middle right, bottom centre, bottom right, middle centre (K and X swap, then L, 8, W, 2 move on; in step 3 to 4, W and T swap, then L, 2, X, 8 move on).

Step 2 to 3 follows a second rule: middle centre and middle right swap (2 and L), the bottom right symbol goes to top left (W), the top left symbol goes to bottom centre (X), the bottom centre symbol goes to bottom right (8), and the bottom left symbol is replaced by a new one (K becomes T).

Step 4 to 5 repeats the second rule on box 4: L goes to middle centre and 8 to middle right, X (bottom right) goes to top left, T (top left) goes to bottom centre, 2 (bottom centre) goes to bottom right, and the bottom left symbol W is replaced by a new one.

Box 5 is X at top left, L and 8 in the middle row, a new symbol at bottom left, T at bottom centre and 2 at bottom right: option 4 (X; L, 8; 9, T, 2). Option 3 has T and W in the wrong bottom places, and options 1 and 2 misplace 2, 8 and T.

chapter: Coding-Decoding
subtopic: Reversal and Opposite Letters
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
In a certain code language, 'BELL' is written as 'MKFA' and 'BOND' is written as 'EMPA'. How will 'BLEW' be written in that language?
A: XDMA
B: XMDA
C: XMAD
D: XDAM
solution:
Write the word backwards, then move the letters +1, -1, +1, -1.

BELL backwards is LLEB: L+1 = M, L-1 = K, E+1 = F, B-1 = A, giving MKFA.

BOND backwards is DNOB: D+1 = E, N-1 = M, O+1 = P, B-1 = A, giving EMPA.

BLEW backwards is WELB: W+1 = X, E-1 = D, L+1 = M, B-1 = A, giving XDMA.

10 is related to 15 following certain logic. Following the same logic, 42 is related to 63. To which of the following numbers is 22 related, following the same logic? (NOTE : Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into its constituent digits. subtopic: Opposite Faces of a Dice
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B

stem:
Six letters S, T, M, P, R and C are written on different faces of a dice. Two positions of this dice are shown in the figures below. Find the letter on the face opposite to M.
A: C
B: R
C: S
D: P
solution:
The first position shows T on top with S and M on the side faces; the second shows T on top with P and R on the side faces.

T touches S, M, P and R, so C is opposite T, and S, M, P and R form the ring of four faces around T.
