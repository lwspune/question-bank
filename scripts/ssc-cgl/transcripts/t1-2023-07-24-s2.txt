=== 1
chapter: Mathematical Operations
subtopic: Interchanging Signs and Numbers
difficulty: EASY
answer: D
stem:
Which two signs should be interchanged to make the given equation correct?
\(17 + 10 \times 5 \div 9 + 18 = 53\)
A: + and \(\div\)
B: + and \(\times\)
C: + and \(-\)
D: \(\times\) and \(\div\)
solution:
Interchange \(\times\) and \(\div\): \(17 + 10 \div 5 \times 9 + 18 = 17 + 2 \times 9 + 18 = 17 + 18 + 18 = 53\). Correct.

Check the others: + and \(\div\) gives \(17 \div 10 \times 5 + 9 \div 18 = 8.5 + 0.5 = 9\); + and \(\times\) gives \(17 \times 10 + 5 \div 9 \times 18 = 170 + 10 = 180\); the equation has no minus sign, so + and \(-\) only turns both + into \(-\), which does not give 53.

chapter: Non-Verbal Series and Analogy
subtopic: Figure Series
difficulty: HARD
answer: B
figure: stem+options
stem:
Select the figure that will replace the question mark (?) in the following figure series.
A: [figure]
B: [figure]
C: [figure]
D: [figure]
solution:
Each figure is a grid of 3 rows and 4 columns of symbols. The figures read row by row are:

Figure 1: B O 3 S / \(\square\) A X \(\uparrow\) / U Z = L

Figure 2: U A O B / \(\uparrow\) Z 3 \(\square\) / L = X S

Figure 3: A Z B \(\square\) / U \(\uparrow\) O 3 / S L = X

Figure 4: S \(\uparrow\) Z A / 3 L B U / X = O \(\square\)

The moves from figure 3 to figure 4 are exactly the moves from figure 1 to figure 2, so the steps alternate between two fixed rearrangements. The next step repeats the move from figure 2 to figure 3: the top-left symbol goes down one place, the second symbol of the top row goes to the top-left, the third goes down one, the fourth goes left one, the first of the middle row goes right one, the second of the middle row goes up one, the third goes right one, the fourth goes up one, and the bottom row shifts one place right, its last symbol going to the front.

Applied to figure 4 this gives \(\uparrow\) L A U / S 3 Z B / \(\square\) X = O, which is option 2.

A paper is folded and cut as shown below. How will it appear when unfolded?
A: [figure]
B: [figure]

solution:

chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Three statements are followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. You have to consider these statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts. Decide which of the given conclusions logically follow(s) from the given statements.

Statements:
All peacocks are pigeons.
Some pigeons are pinks.
Some pinks are birds.

Conclusions:
(I) Some pinks are peacocks.
(II) Some birds are pigeons.
(III) Some peacocks are birds.
A: None of the conclusion follow
B: Either conclusion I or conclusion III follows
C: Only conclusion II follows
D: Only conclusion I follows
solution:
The pigeons that are pinks need not be peacocks, so I does not follow. The pinks that are birds need not be pigeons, so II does not follow, and peacocks have no definite link to birds, so III does not follow.

I and III are about different pairs of terms, so they are not an either-or pair. None of the conclusions follows.

stem:
Three statements are given, followed by four conclusions numbered I, II, III and IV. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
