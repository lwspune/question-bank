subtopic: Medians and Apollonius Theorem
difficulty: EASY
answer: A
stem:
In \(\Delta\)ABC, D and E are the mid points of sides BC and AC, respectively. If AD = 10.8 cm, BE = 14.4 cm and AD and BE intersect at G at a right angle, then the area (in \(\text{cm}^2\)) of \(\Delta\)ABC is:
A: 103.68
B: 56.76
C: 80.64
D: 53.76
solution:
AD and BE are medians, so G is the centroid: \(AG = \frac{2}{3} \times 10.8 = 7.2\) and \(BG = \frac{2}{3} \times 14.4 = 9.6\).

Check with the rule for perpendicular medians: area \(= \frac{2}{3} \times 10.8 \times 14.4 = 103.68\).

=== 6
chapter: Trigonometric Ratios and Identities
subtopic: Simplifying and Proving Identities
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
The value of \(\frac{\sec^2\theta(2+\tan^2\theta+\cot^2\theta)\div(\sin^2\theta-\tan^2\theta)}{(\text{cosec}^2\theta+\sec^2\theta)(1+\cot^2\theta)^2}\) is:
A: 2
B: \(-2\)
C: \(-1\)
D: 1
solution:
\(2 + \tan^2\theta + \cot^2\theta = (\tan\theta + \cot\theta)^2 = \sec^2\theta\,\text{cosec}^2\theta\), so the first part is \(\sec^4\theta\,\text{cosec}^2\theta\).

\(\sin^2\theta - \tan^2\theta = \sin^2\theta(1 - \sec^2\theta) = -\sin^2\theta\tan^2\theta = -\frac{\sin^4\theta}{\cos^2\theta}\).

Numerator \(= -\frac{1}{\cos^4\theta\sin^2\theta} \times \frac{\cos^2\theta}{\sin^4\theta} = -\frac{1}{\cos^2\theta\sin^6\theta}\).

Denominator \(= \frac{1}{\sin^2\theta\cos^2\theta} \times \frac{1}{\sin^4\theta} = \frac{1}{\cos^2\theta\sin^6\theta}\). Value \(= -1\).

Check at \(\theta = 45^\circ\): numerator \(2 \times 4 \div (\frac{1}{2} - 1) = -16\); denominator \(4 \times 4 = 16\); value \(-1\).
