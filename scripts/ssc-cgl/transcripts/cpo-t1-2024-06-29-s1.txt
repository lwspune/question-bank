chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Two-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
flawed: yes
notice: The two conclusions form a complementary pair, so strictly "either I or II follows", which no option offers; neither follows on its own.
note: Conclusions I (no network is a wire) and II (some networks are wires) are a complementary pair; "either I or II" is not offered, so "Neither follows" (also SSC's key) is the closest option.
stem:
Two statements are given followed by two conclusions numbered I and II. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.
Statements:
1) All networks are fibers.
2) Some fibers are wires.
Conclusions:
I. All networks can never be wires.
II. Some networks are wires.
A: Neither Conclusion I nor II follows.
B: Both Conclusions I and II follow.
C: Only Conclusion I follows.
D: Only Conclusion II follows.
solution:
The wires that are fibers may or may not include any network, so neither "no network is a wire" (I) nor "some networks are wires" (II) is certain on its own.

The two conclusions form a complementary pair, so either I or II would follow, but that choice is not offered. Taken one at a time, neither follows.
