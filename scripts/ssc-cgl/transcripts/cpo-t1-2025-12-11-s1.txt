stem:
If 1 + 2 = 4, 2 + 4 = 10 , then 3 + 4 = ?
A: 16
B: 14
C: 12
D: 13
solution:
Rule: multiply the two numbers and add 2. \(1 \times 2 + 2 = 4\) and \(2 \times 4 + 2 = 10\).

So \(3 \times 4 + 2 = 14\).

The examples also fit "square of the first number, plus both numbers": \(1 + 1 + 2 = 4\), \(4 + 2 + 4 = 10\), which would give \(9 + 3 + 4 = 16\). The product-plus-2 rule is the simpler one, so 14.

A: The man asked if I did not realize how serious this was.
B: The man asked me did I not realize how serious it was.
C: The man told I don’t realize how serious this is.
D: The man asked whether I did realize how serious this is.
solution:
A yes/no question is reported with "asked" + "if/whether", in statement order, with the tense shifted back: "do you not realize" becomes "I did not realize", and "is" becomes "was". Option A does this. B keeps question order ("did I not"), C uses "told" and no tense shift, and D drops the "not" and keeps "is".

stem:
Choose the most suitable option to replace the underlined part of the sentence:
The scientist explained the phenomenon ***in layman’s language***.
A: in the language of layman
B: using ordinary terms
C: in layman's terms
D: to ordinary people
solution:
The standard idiom is "in layman's terms", meaning in simple words that a non-expert can follow. "In the language of layman" lacks an article and is not idiomatic, while "using ordinary terms" and "to ordinary people" drop the fixed expression the sentence is reaching for.
