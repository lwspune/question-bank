=== 1
chapter: Coding-Decoding
subtopic: Reversal and Opposite Letters
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
In a certain code language, 'QUANTITY' is written as 'TPMZHSXS', 'RELIABLE' is written as 'DQHKAZDK'. How will 'SAMPLING' be written in that language?
A: ZROLHLGM
B: ZROLHJEL
C: ZROLHKFM
D: ZROLHKFN
solution:
Split the word into pairs, swap the two letters of each pair, then move every letter one place back.

QU AN TI TY becomes UQ NA IT YT, and one place back gives TP MZ HS XS: TPMZHSXS.

RE LI AB LE becomes ER IL BA EL, and one place back gives DQ HK AZ DK: DQHKAZDK.

SA MP LI NG becomes AS PM IL GN, and one place back gives ZR OL HK FM: ZROLHKFM.

subtopic: Letter Position and Alphabet Test
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Each vowel in the word "FRETSAW" is changed to the following letter in the English alphabetical series and each consonant is changed to the previous letter in the English alphabetical series. In the newly formed word, how many alphabets are there in the English alphabetical series between the alphabet which is \(3^{\text{rd}}\) from the right and \(1^{\text{st}}\) from the right?
A: Three
B: Four
C: One
D: Two
solution:
F, R, T, S, W go one letter back (E, Q, S, R, V) and the vowels E, A go one letter forward (F, B). FRETSAW becomes E Q F S R B V.

The 3rd letter from the right is R and the 1st from the right is V. Between R and V come S, T and U: three letters.

stem:
Which of the following numbers will replace the question mark (?) in the given series?
11, ?, 29, 53, 101, 197
A: 19
B: 13
C: 21
D: 17
solution:
Each term is twice the previous term minus 5: \(29 \times 2 - 5 = 53\), \(53 \times 2 - 5 = 101\), \(101 \times 2 - 5 = 197\).

So the missing term is \(11 \times 2 - 5 = 17\), and \(17 \times 2 - 5 = 29\) checks. The differences 6, 12, 24, 48, 96 double each time.

subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: A
stem:
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
All leathers are shoes.
Some bags are shoes.
Some bags are papers.

Conclusions:
I. Some papers are leathers.
II. Some shoes are leathers.
III. Some shoes are papers.
A: Only conclusion II follows
B: Only conclusion I follows
C: Only conclusion III follows
D: All conclusions follow
solution:
All leathers are shoes, so some shoes are leathers: II follows.

=== 12
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: A
stem:
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
Some hills are mountains.
Some hills are valleys.
All valleys are rivers.

Conclusions:
I. Some rivers are valleys.
II. Some mountains are valleys.
III. Some hills are rivers.
A: Only conclusions I and III follow
B: Only conclusions II and III follow
C: Only conclusions I and II follow
D: Only conclusion I follows
solution:
All valleys are rivers, so some rivers are valleys: I follows.

Some hills are valleys and all valleys are rivers, so those hills are rivers: III follows.

Mountains and valleys are linked only through hills, and the two groups of hills need not overlap: II does not follow. I and III follow.
