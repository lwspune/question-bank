Which of the following numbers will replace the question marks (?) in the given series?
16, 36, 64, ?, ?, 196, 256, 324
A: 81, 100
B: 81, 121
C: 100, 121
D: 100, 144
solution:

chapter: Mathematical Operations
subtopic: Operations on Number Sets
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
Two sets of numbers are given below. In each set of numbers, certain mathematical operation(s) on the first number result(s) in the second number. Similarly, certain mathematical operation(s) on the second number result(s) in the third number and so on. Which of the given options follows the same set of operations as in the given sets?

(NOTE: Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into their constituent digits. E.g. 13 – Operations on 13 such as adding/subtracting/multiplying to 13 can be performed. Breaking down 13 into 1 and 3 and then performing mathematical operations on 1 and 3 is not allowed.)

10 – 12 – 8 – 15; 12 – 14 – 10 – 19
A: 8 – 6 – 10 – 18
B: 11 – 13 – 9 – 17
C: 6 – 8 – 12 – 16
D: 15 – 13 – 9 – 19
solution:
The rule is add 2, then subtract 4, then double and subtract 1: \(10 + 2 = 12\), \(12 - 4 = 8\), \(8 \times 2 - 1 = 15\); and \(12 + 2 = 14\), \(14 - 4 = 10\), \(10 \times 2 - 1 = 19\).

Option B: \(11 + 2 = 13\), \(13 - 4 = 9\), \(9 \times 2 - 1 = 17\). It fits.

The others fail at the first step: 8 to 6, 6 to 8 then 8 to 12 (should be 4), 15 to 13.

stem:
Which figure should replace the question mark (?) if the following series were to be continued?
A: [figure]
B: [figure]
C: [figure]
D: [figure]
solution:
Each figure has three rows of symbols. The figures go in pairs: from the first to the second, and from the third to the fourth, the rows only move up one place (the top row goes to the bottom).

From the second to the third the rows keep their places, the top row loses one symbol, the middle row doubles and the bottom row gains one: OOO, XX, B become OO, XXXX, BB.

Based on the English alphabetical order, three of the following four letter-clusters are alike in a certain way and thus form a group. Which is the letter-cluster that DOES NOT belong to that group?

chapter: Blood Relations
subtopic: Coded Relations
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: D
stem:
In a certain code language,
A + B means ‘A is the son of B’,
A − B means ‘A is the wife of B’,
A \(\times\) B means ‘A is the brother of B’ and
A \(\div\) B means ‘A is the daughter of B’.
Based on the above, how is P related to R if ‘P \(\times\) S \(\div\) M − T + R’?
A: Daughter
B: Brother
C: Father’s mother
D: Son’s son
solution:
P \(\times\) S: P is the brother of S. S \(\div\) M: S is the daughter of M. M − T: M is the wife of T. T + R: T is the son of R.

So P is a son of M and T, and T is a son of R. P is R's son's son.

=== 9
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: C
stem:
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:

All cards are postcards.
Some postcards are books.
All books are novels.

Conclusions:

I. Some postcards are novels.
II. No card is a book.
III. All cards are novels.
A: Only II and III conclusion follow
B: Only I and II conclusion follow
C: Only conclusion I follows
D: Only conclusion III follows
solution:
Some postcards are books and all books are novels, so those postcards are novels: I follows.

Cards may or may not overlap with books, so II does not follow.

Cards are known to be postcards only, and nothing puts all postcards inside novels, so III does not follow. Only I follows.

Select the combination of letters that when sequentially placed in the blanks of the given series will complete the series.

E_GHEI_GELK_ _OME
A: IFGE
B: IGFE
C: FIFE
D: FGEI
solution:
Split the series into four groups of four: E_GH, EI_G, ELK_, _OME. Each group starts with E.

Second letters go F, I, L, O (+3), so the first blank is F. Third letters go G, I, K, M (+2), so the second blank is I. Fourth letters go H, G, F, E (−1), so the third blank is F. The last group starts with E.

The blanks are F, I, F, E: FIFE, giving EFGH EIIG ELKF EOME.
