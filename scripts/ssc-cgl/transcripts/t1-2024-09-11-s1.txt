Which two numbers (not digits) should be interchanged to make the given equation correct?
\(48 \div 3 + 12 \times 4 - 6 \times 7 + 21 = 27\)

B: 4 and 7
C: 3 and 4
D: 3 and 6

Check the others: 12 and 21 gives \(16 + 84 - 42 + 12 = 70\); 4 and 7 gives \(16 + 84 - 24 + 21 = 97\); 3 and 6 gives \(8 + 48 - 18 + 21 = 59\).

chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: A
stem:
Three statements are given, followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
All bottles are jugs.
Some bottles are flasks.
All flasks are bins.

Conclusions:
I. Some bottles are bins.
II. Some jugs are bins.
III. Some flasks are jugs.
A: All conclusions I, II and III follow
B: Only conclusions II and III follow
C: Either conclusion I or II follows
D: Only conclusions I and II follow
solution:
The bottles that are flasks are also bins (all flasks are bins), so some bottles are bins: I follows.

Those same bottles are jugs (all bottles are jugs), so some jugs are bins: II follows, and some flasks are jugs: III follows.

All three follow.

chapter: Blood Relations
subtopic: Coded Statements and Expressions
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
In a certain code language,
A + B means ‘A is the son of B’,
A − B means ‘A is the wife of B’,
A \(\times\) B means ‘A is the brother of B’ and
A \(\div\) B means ‘A is the mother of B’.
Based on the above, if ‘P + Q \(\div\) R − T \(\times\) M’, which of the following is true?
A: T is the brother of P.
B: M is the sister of T.
C: P is the brother of R.
D: Q is the mother of M.
solution:
P + Q: P is the son of Q. Q \(\div\) R: Q is the mother of R. R − T: R is the wife of T. T \(\times\) M: T is the brother of M.

P and R are both children of Q, and P is male, so P is the brother of R.

T is P's brother-in-law, M's gender is not known, and Q is not M's mother.
