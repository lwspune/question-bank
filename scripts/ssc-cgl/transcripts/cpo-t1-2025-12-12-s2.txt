subtopic: Unfolding a Quarter
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C

stem:
A paper is folded and cut as shown in the following figures. How will it appear when unfolded?
A: [figure]
B: [figure]
C: [figure]
D: [figure]
solution:

chapter: Statement-Based Reasoning
subtopic: Inferences
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
**Instruction:** Choose the inference that are clearly supported by the statement.
**Statement:** Participants in the advanced mindfulness program reported significantly lower stress levels after completing the 8-week course compared to their pre-course assessments.
**Inferences:**
Inference I: The advanced mindfulness program is an effective method for reducing stress.
Inference II: The reduction in stress levels among participants is solely due to the content of the 8-week course.
A: Only Inference I follows
B: Only Inference II follows
C: Both Inferences I and II follow
D: Neither Inference I nor II follows
solution:
Participants reported significantly lower stress after the course than before it, so the statement supports the program being an effective way to reduce stress: Inference I follows.

Inference II claims the drop is due solely to the course content. The word "solely" rules out every other factor (time, other changes in their lives, the group setting), and nothing in the statement supports that. So only I follows.

subtopic: Statement and Conclusions
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
**Which of the following option, if true, would most undermine the conclusion?**
**Observation:** A large number of street vendors moved from the city market area to the main railway station area. **Conclusion:** The City Market Authority must have enforced stricter regulations, causing the vendors to relocate.
A: Rent in the railway station area is significantly higher than in the city market area.

chapter: Figure Counting
subtopic: Counting Triangles
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
figure: stem
stem:
Find the number of triangles in the given figure.
A: 11
B: 13
C: 15
D: 17
solution:
Name the rectangle's corners A (top left), B (top right), C (bottom right) and D (bottom left). The diagonals AC and BD cross at O. A point P lies on the diagonal AC between O and C, and P is joined to D and to B.

So there are six points, A, B, C, D, O and P, and every pair of them is joined by a drawn line. That gives \(\binom{6}{3} = 20\) sets of three points.

The only sets that do not make a triangle are three points on one line: on AC the points A, O, P, C give \(\binom{4}{3} = 4\) such sets, and on BD the points B, O, D give 1. So there are 20 - 4 - 1 = 15 triangles.

chapter: Figure Counting
subtopic: Counting Triangles
difficulty: HARD
answer: D
figure: stem
stem:
Find the number of triangles in the given figure.
A: 22
B: 24
C: 26
D: 28
solution:
The figure is a star of seven straight lines drawn between six points on a circle (the circle adds no triangles). Call the points on the circle T1 (top left), T2 (top right), L (left), R (right), B1 (bottom left) and B2 (bottom right). The lines are T1B1, T1B2, T2B1, T2B2, LR (horizontal), LB2 and RB1.

Every triangle is made by three of the seven lines that meet each other pairwise at three different points. There are \(\binom{7}{3} = 35\) sets of three lines.

Only one pair of lines never meets: T1B1 and T2B2 (the two near-vertical sides). The 5 sets containing both of them make no triangle.

Two sets have all three lines through one point: T1B1, T2B1 and RB1 all pass through B1, and T1B2, T2B2 and LB2 all pass through B2. They make no triangle either.

Every other pair of lines meets at its own point, so the triangles number 35 - 5 - 2 = 28.

subtopic: Cause, Effect and Courses of Action
difficulty: EASY
answer: D
stem:
You observe a large amount of smoke billowing from a factory smokestack late at night, and you suspect they are illegally burning unauthorised waste because the smoke smells toxic. What is your primary course of action?
A: Ignore it, as it's probably just steam from their machinery.
B: Call the fire department immediately since it involves fire/smoke.
C: Call the factory security or the night manager to inquire about the situation.
D: Collect evidence (like a photo) and report the incident to the local or state environmental quality agency.

Choose the correct indirect speech of the sentence:
She said, “Why didn’t anyone inform me about the change?”

solution:
A reported question uses "asked", keeps the question word "why", and takes statement order (subject before verb). === 199
chapter: Grammar
subtopic: Direct and Indirect Speech
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
blind: B
flawed: yes
notice: A negative question can be reported with or without its negative; the answer shown is SSC's key.
note: Judgement item: our blind answer B (keeping the negative, "whether I didn't understand") and SSC's key C (the question reported plainly, "if I understood") both report it. SSC's key is adopted.
stem:
Choose the correct indirect speech of the sentence:
She said, “Don’t you understand the seriousness of the matter?”
A: She asked why I didn’t understand the serious matter.
B: She asked whether I didn’t understand the seriousness of the matter.
C: She asked if I understood the seriousness of the matter.
D: She asked do I understand how serious it is.
solution:
A yes/no question is reported with "asked if/whether" in statement order, with "you" becoming "I" and the present moving back to the past. A negative question like "Don't you understand ...?" is usually reported plainly, without the negative: She asked if I understood the seriousness of the matter. That is SSC's key.

Keeping the negative, "whether I didn't understand" (B), is the other reading, which is why the question carries a note. A adds "why" and changes the words, and D keeps question order and the present tense. The answer is: She asked if I understood the seriousness of the matter.

Choose the most suitable option to replace the highlighted part of the sentence:
He is **more wiser than his brother.**
A: very wise than
B: more wise than
