notice: SSC's printed paper shows this question in Hindi only; the English here is our translation.

stem:
If,
'B % D' means 'B is the brother of D',
'B & D' means 'B is the mother of D',
'B \(\times\) D' means 'B is the husband of D',
'B # D' means 'B is the sister of D',
'B \(\text{\textdollar}\) D' means 'B is the son of D',
'B @ D' means 'B is the father of D',
then how is A related to E in the given expression?
A % D \(\text{\textdollar}\) F # B % E \(\times\) S
A: Husband
B: Sister's son (nephew)
C: Mother's brother (maternal uncle)
D: Brother
solution:
A % D: A is the brother of D. D \(\text{\textdollar}\) F: D is the son of F, so A is also F's son.

F # B: F is the sister of B. B % E: B is the brother of E. So F is E's sister.

E \(\times\) S: E is the husband of S, so E is male.

A is the son of E's sister, so A is E's nephew (bhanja).

difficulty: EASY
answer: C
stem:
Three statements are given, followed by four conclusions numbered I, II, III and IV. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
Some trees are leaves.
Some leaves are flowers.
Some flowers are branches.

Conclusions:
I. Some trees are flowers.
II. Some leaves are branches.
III. Some branches are flowers.
IV. Some branches are leaves.
A: Only conclusions II and III follow
B: Only conclusions I and II follow
C: Only conclusion III follows
D: None of the conclusions follow
solution:
"Some flowers are branches" converts to "Some branches are flowers", so III follows.

Two "some" statements in a chain give no conclusion, so trees and flowers (I), leaves and branches (II, IV) have no definite link. Only III follows.
