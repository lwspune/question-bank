=== 1
chapter: Figure Counting
subtopic: Counting Squares and Other Figures
difficulty: HARD
answer: A
figure: stem
stem:
How many squares are there in the given figure?
A: 20
B: 17
C: 19
D: 18
solution:
The figure has a staircase of small unit squares: 2 in the top row, 4 in the second and 4 in the third, which makes 10.

Squares of side 2 units: the one made by the four cells in the upper two rows, the two in the middle and right of the lower two rows (with the outer frame closing them), the one at the right edge of the lower two rows, the two formed by the lower row and the bottom strip, and the one at the top right of the frame. That is 7.

At the top left there are 3 more: the separate square, the square drawn at the top left corner of the frame, and the small square where these two overlap.

Total \(= 10 + 7 + 3 = 20\).

34 is related to 191 by certain logic. Following the same logic, 56 is related to 301. To which of the following is 63 related, following the same logic?

chapter: Blood Relations
subtopic: Coded Relations
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
In a certain code language,

'\(A + B\)' means 'A is the mother of B';
'\(A - B\)' means 'A is the brother of B';
'\(A \times B\)' means 'A is the wife of B' and
'\(A \div B\)' means 'A is the father of B'.

Based on the above, how is 5 related to 2 if '\(5 \div 1 \times 3 \div 4 - 2\)'?
A: Mother's mother
B: Father's father
C: Mother's father
D: Father's mother
solution:
\(5 \div 1\): 5 is the father of 1. \(1 \times 3\): 1 is the wife of 3. \(3 \div 4\): 3 is the father of 4. \(4 - 2\): 4 is the brother of 2.

So 2 is a child of 3 and of his wife 1, which makes 1 the mother of 2. 5 is the father of 1, so 5 is 2's mother's father.

stem:
The chemical formula of glucose is \(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6\). The weight % of carbon in glucose is:
A: 53
B: 25
C: 72
D: 40
solution:
Molar mass of glucose \(= 6 \times 12 + 12 \times 1 + 6 \times 16 = 72 + 12 + 96 = 180\) g/mol.

Mass of carbon \(= 72\) g, so carbon \(= \frac{72}{180} \times 100 = 40\%\).

Check: oxygen is \(\frac{96}{180} \times 100 = 53.3\%\) and hydrogen \(6.7\%\); \(40 + 53.3 + 6.7 = 100\).
