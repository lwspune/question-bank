chapter: Time and Work
subtopic: Pipes and Cisterns
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: D
stem:
There are three taps of diameter 2 cm, 3 cm and 4 cm, respectively. The ratio of the water flowing through them is equal to the ratio of the square of their diameters. The biggest tap can fill an empty tank alone in 81 min. If all the taps are opened simultaneously, then how long will the tank take (in min) to be filled?
A: \(34\frac{20}{29}\)
B: \(64\frac{20}{29}\)
C: \(54\frac{20}{29}\)
D: \(44\frac{20}{29}\)
solution:
Flow rates are in the ratio \(2^2 : 3^2 : 4^2 = 4 : 9 : 16\).

The biggest tap (16 units a minute) fills the tank in 81 min, so the tank holds \(16 \times 81 = 1296\) units.

All three together give \(4 + 9 + 16 = 29\) units a minute, so the time is \(\frac{1296}{29} = 44\frac{20}{29}\) min.

Check: \(29 \times 44 = 1276\) and \(1296 - 1276 = 20\).

Find the mode for the given distribution (rounded off to two decimal places).

| Class Interval | 5 \(-\) 10 | 10 \(-\) 15 | 15 \(-\) 20 | 20 \(-\) 25 | 25 \(-\) 30 | 30 \(-\) 35 |

chapter: Data Sufficiency
subtopic: Data Sufficiency
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
Given below is a question followed by two statements I and II. Read both the statements carefully to decide which one of them is sufficient to answer the question.
Six people - K, M, N, P, Q and R are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. Who sits to the immediate right of K?
(I) K sits third to the left of M. Only one person sits between K and N. R sits to the immediate left of N.
(II) K sits second to the right of Q. Only two people sit between K and M. P sits second to the left of R.
A: Data in statement I alone is sufficient to answer the question while data in statement II is not.
B: Either data in statement I or statement II is sufficient to answer the question.
C: Data in statements I and II together are sufficient to answer the question.
D: Data in statement II alone is sufficient to answer the question while data in statement I is not.
solution:
Number the seats 0 to 5 going to the left (for people facing the centre, left is clockwise). Put K at 0; the immediate right of K is seat 5.

Statement I: K is third to the left of M, so M is directly opposite, at 3. N is at 2 or 4 (one person between). R is immediately left of N, so R is at N + 1. N = 2 would put R at 3, which is M's seat, so N = 4 and R = 5. R is to the immediate right of K. Statement I alone is enough.

Statement II: K is second to the right of Q, so Q is at 2. Two people between K and M puts M at 3. N, P and R take seats 1, 4 and 5, with P two to the left of R (P = R + 2). Only R = 5, P = 1 works, leaving N at 4. Again R is to the immediate right of K. Statement II alone is enough.

Either statement alone answers the question.
