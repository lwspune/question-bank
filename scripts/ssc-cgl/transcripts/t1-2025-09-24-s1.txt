12, 34, 100, 298, 894, 2674

solution:

subtopic: Word-Code Sentences
difficulty: MODERATE

stem:
In a certain code language, 'trick on him twice' is coded as 'wl, dl, kl, sl', 'him slow twice' is coded as 'kl, jl, sl', 'case on him' is coded as 'pl, dl, kl'. How will 'on slow trick' be coded in the same language?
A: dl, kl, wl
B: dl, jl, wl
C: pl, jl, wl
D: dl, sl, wl
solution:
'him' is in all three sentences and 'kl' is in all three codes: him = kl.

Sentences 1 and 3 share 'on' and 'him', codes share 'dl' and 'kl': on = dl.

Sentences 1 and 2 share 'him' and 'twice', codes share 'kl' and 'sl': twice = sl.

Then sentence 1 gives trick = wl, and sentence 2 gives slow = jl.

'on slow trick' = dl, jl, wl.

Statement:
No tables are chairs. All chairs are wooden.

I. Some wooden things are not tables.
II. No tables are wooden.
A: Only I follows
B: Only II follows
C: Both I and II follow
D: Neither I nor II follows

I. Some men are hardworking.

B: Only II follows
