D: [figure]
solution:

=== 9
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Three Statements are given followed by Three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
All pens are newspapers.
Some newspapers are novels.
No novel is a book.

Conclusions:
I. Some books are pens.
II. Some novels are pens.
III. No book is a pen.
A: Either conclusion I or III follows.
B: Both conclusions II and III follow.
C: Either conclusion II or III follows.
D: Both conclusions I and II follow.
solution:
Pens sit inside newspapers, and the newspapers that are novels need not include any pen, so II (some novels are pens) does not definitely follow.

Books are kept away from novels only; nothing links books with pens, so neither I nor III follows on its own.

I (some books are pens) and III (no book is a pen) are about the same two classes and one of them must be true, so they form an either-or pair: either I or III follows.

subtopic: Number Set Analogy
difficulty: EASY
answer: D
stem:
Select the set in which the numbers are related in the same way as are the numbers of the given sets.
(NOTE : Operations should be performed on the whole numbers, without breaking down the numbers into its constituent digits. E.g. 13 – Operations on 13 such as adding /Subtracting /multiplying etc. to 13 can be performed. Breaking down 13 into 1 and 3 and then performing mathematical operations on 1 and 3 is NOT allowed)

(38, 49, 60)

C: (25, 69, 45)
D: (14, 24, 34)

The other sets: 63, 60, 65 goes down then up; 15, 20, 31 rises by 5 then 11; 25, 69, 45 goes up then down.

The country can improve its balance of payments by devaluation when the sum of elasticity of demand for exports and imports is ________.

B: equal to unity
C: greater than unity
D: zero
solution:
This is the Marshall-Lerner condition: devaluation improves the trade balance only if the sum of the price elasticities of demand for exports and imports is greater than one. If the sum is below one the balance worsens, and at exactly one it is unchanged.
