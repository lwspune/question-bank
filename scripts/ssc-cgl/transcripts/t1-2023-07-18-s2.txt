=== 6
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: B
stem:
Three statements are given followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
All fruits are wines.
Some wines are juices.
All juices are alcohols.

Conclusions:
I. Some alcohols are juices.
II. Some wines are alcohols.
III. Some wines are fruits.
A: Only conclusions I and II follow
B: All conclusions follow
C: Only conclusions I and III follow
D: Only conclusions II and III follow
solution:
I: All juices are alcohols, so some alcohols are juices. Follows.

II: Some wines are juices and all juices are alcohols, so those wines are alcohols. Follows.

III: All fruits are wines, so some wines are fruits. Follows.

All three follow.

=== 23
chapter: Coding-Decoding
subtopic: Reversal and Opposite Letters
difficulty: HARD
answer: A
stem:
In a certain code language, ‘PLACE’ is written as ‘CONHA’ and ‘SHOCK’ is written as ‘QKQNA’. How will ‘FROWN’ be written in that language?
A: QUDQU
B: RVRST
C: RUSRT
D: QVQDU
solution:
Reverse the first three letters and the last two, then shift the letters by +2, +3, \(-2\), +3, \(-2\).

PLACE: ALP EC, then A+2 = C, L+3 = O, P\(-2\) = N, E+3 = H, C\(-2\) = A: CONHA.

SHOCK: OHS KC gives Q, K, Q, N, A: QKQNA.

FROWN: ORF NW gives O+2 = Q, R+3 = U, F\(-2\) = D, N+3 = Q, W\(-2\) = U: QUDQU.

"Be busy" describes a state, so it takes the simple present: "I am really busy." "Am being" is used for temporary behaviour (he is being rude), not for a state like being busy.
