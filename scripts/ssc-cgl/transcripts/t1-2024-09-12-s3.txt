B: OQGPO
C: NQGPR

A: [figure]
B: [figure]

solution:

=== 3
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
Three statements are given, followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. Assuming the statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts, decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s) from the statements.

Statements:
No lotus is a daisy.
All lotuses are tulips.
All tulips are sunflowers.

Conclusions:
I. All daisies are tulips.
II. Some sunflowers are daisies.
III. No sunflower is a daisy.
A: Only conclusion II follows
B: Either conclusion II or III follows
C: Only conclusion I follows
D: Only conclusion III follows
solution:
Lotus lies inside tulip, which lies inside sunflower; daisy is only kept apart from lotus. Daisy may or may not overlap tulip or sunflower.

I is not definite: daisies need not be tulips at all.

II and III are not definite on their own, but they speak of the same two terms (sunflower and daisy), one saying "some" and the other "no". Exactly one of them must be true, so they form an either-or pair: either II or III follows.

chapter: Mathematical Operations
subtopic: Interchanging Signs and Numbers
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: D
stem:
Which of the following interchanges of numbers (not digits) would make the given equation correct?
\(2^{57-33\div11\times13-2}+3^{64-56\div8\times4-44}=35\)
A: 2 and 4
B: 56 and 57
C: 57 and 64
D: 33 and 44
solution:
Interchange 33 and 44: \(2^{57-44\div11\times13-2}+3^{64-56\div8\times4-33}\).

First exponent: \(44\div11=4\), \(4\times13=52\), \(57-52-2=3\), so \(2^3=8\).

Second exponent: \(56\div8=7\), \(7\times4=28\), \(64-28-33=3\), so \(3^3=27\).

\(8+27=35\). Correct.

Check the others: interchanging 56 and 57 or 57 and 64 leaves the first exponent at 15 or 23, far too large; interchanging 2 and 4 makes the first term \(4^{14}\).

chapter: Blood Relations
subtopic: Coded Relations
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: B
stem:
In a certain code language,

‘\(A + B\)’ means ‘A is the mother of B’;
‘\(A - B\)’ means ‘A is the husband of B’;
‘\(A \times B\)’ means ‘A is the father of B’ and
‘\(A \div B\)’ means ‘A is the daughter of B’.

Based on the above, how is K related to O if ‘\(K \div L - M + N \times O\)’?
A: Sister’s daughter
B: Father’s sister
C: Mother’s sister
D: Father’s mother
solution:
\(K \div L\): K is the daughter of L. \(L - M\): L is the husband of M. \(M + N\): M is the mother of N. \(N \times O\): N is the father of O.

L and M are the parents of both K and N, so K is N's sister. N is O's father, so K is O's father's sister.
