=== 2
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: EASY
answer: C
stem:
Read the given statements and conclusions carefully. You have to take the given statements to be true even if they seem to be at variance from commonly known facts. You have to decide which conclusion/s logically follow/s from the given statements.
Statements:
All apples are mangoes.
All mangoes are lemons.
All lemons are kiwis.
Conclusions:
(I) All apples are lemons.
(II) All mangoes are kiwis.
A: Neither conclusion I nor II follow.
B: Only conclusion II follows.
C: Both conclusions I and II follow.
D: Only conclusion I follows.
solution:
The statements form a chain: apples inside mangoes, mangoes inside lemons, lemons inside kiwis.

All apples are mangoes and all mangoes are lemons, so all apples are lemons: I follows.

All mangoes are lemons and all lemons are kiwis, so all mangoes are kiwis: II follows. Both follow.

B: CTYF

=== 6
chapter: Syllogism
subtopic: Three-Statement Syllogism
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Three statements are followed by three conclusions numbered I, II and III. You have to consider these statements to be true, even if they seem to be at variance with commonly known facts. Decide which of the given conclusions logically follow/s from the given statements.

Statements:
All pens are pages.
Some pages are pins.
Some pins are boards.

Conclusions:
(I) Some pins are pens.
(II) Some boards are pages.
(III) Some pens are boards.
A: None of the conclusions follow.
B: Either conclusion I or conclusion III follows.
C: Only conclusion I follows.
D: Only conclusion II follows.
solution:
The pins that are pages need not be pens, so I is not definite.

The boards that are pins need not be among the pins that are pages, so II is not definite.

Pens and boards have no link at all, so III is not definite.

I and III are about different pairs of terms, so they cannot form an "either-or" pair. None of the conclusions follows.

chapter: Figure Counting
subtopic: Counting Triangles
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
figure: stem
stem:
How many triangles are there in the given figure?
A: 10
B: 9
C: 12
D: 11
solution:
The figure is two hexagons joined by a small square.

The left hexagon is cut by three lines through its centre (one vertical, two slanting) into 6 triangles. Two or more of them together always make a four- or more-sided shape, so the left hexagon gives 6.

The right hexagon holds a large triangle from its top vertex to its two lower side vertices. That makes the large triangle, the triangle on its left, the one on its right and the one below its base: 4. Any two of them together make a four-sided shape.

Total \(6 + 4 = 10\).

=== 13
chapter: Blood Relations
subtopic: Coded Relations
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
In a certain code language,
A + B means ‘A is the wife of B’.
A \(-\) B means ‘A is the son of B’.
A \(\times\) B means ‘A is the husband of B’.
A \(\div\) B means ‘A is the father of B’.
Based on the above, how is P related to T if ‘P \(-\) Q + R \(\div\) S \(\times\) T’?
A: Husband’s brother
B: Son’s son
C: Father
D: Father-in-law
solution:
P \(-\) Q: P is the son of Q. Q + R: Q is the wife of R. R \(\div\) S: R is the father of S. S \(\times\) T: S is the husband of T.

So P and S are both children of Q and R, and P is male: P is S's brother. S is T's husband, so P is T's husband's brother.

subtopic: International Trade and Finance
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: A
stem:
Match the concepts in column A with their respective descriptions in column B.

| Column A | Column B |
|---|---|
| a. Devaluation | 1. decline in the market rate of exchange |
| b. Depreciation | 2. contraction of home currency |
| c. Deflation | 3. foreign exchange rationing |
| d. Exchange control | 4. contraction of imports |
|  | 5. official reduction in external value of the currency |
A: a-5, b-1, c-2, d-3
B: a-2, b-3, c-4, d-1
C: a-5, b-3, c-2, d-1
D: a-2, b-1, c-4, d-3
solution:
Devaluation is an official (government) reduction in the external value of the currency: a-5.

Depreciation is a fall in the market exchange rate under market forces: b-1.

Deflation contracts the money supply (home currency) and prices: c-2.

Exchange control rations the available foreign exchange: d-3.

So a-5, b-1, c-2, d-3.
