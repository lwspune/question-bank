chapter: Quadratic Equations
subtopic: Forming an Equation from its Roots
difficulty: EASY
answer: D
stem:
The roots of the quadratic equation are \(-2\) and \(\frac{11}{2}\) then the equation is
A: \(x^2 + 5x + 3 = 0\)
B: \(x^2 + 3x + 22 = 0\)
C: \(2x^2 - 4x + 11 = 0\)
D: \(2x^2 - 7x - 22 = 0\)
solution:
Sum of roots \(= -2 + \frac{11}{2} = \frac{7}{2}\). Product of roots \(= -2 \times \frac{11}{2} = -11\).

The equation is \(x^2 - \frac{7}{2}x - 11 = 0\), that is \(2x^2 - 7x - 22 = 0\).

Check: \(2x^2 - 7x - 22 = (x + 2)(2x - 11)\), whose roots are \(-2\) and \(\frac{11}{2}\).

chapter: Linear Equations
subtopic: Fraction Word Problems
difficulty: MODERATE
answer: C
stem:
A fraction becomes \(\frac{1}{3}\) if 1 is subtracted from both the numerator and denominator. If one is added to both the numerator and the denominator it becomes \(\frac{1}{2}\) then the fraction is
A: \(\frac{2}{5}\)
B: \(\frac{3}{5}\)
C: \(\frac{3}{7}\)
D: \(\frac{4}{5}\)
solution:
Let the fraction be \(\frac{x}{y}\).

\(\frac{x - 1}{y - 1} = \frac{1}{3}\) gives \(y = 3x - 2\).

\(\frac{x + 1}{y + 1} = \frac{1}{2}\) gives \(y = 2x + 1\).

So \(3x - 2 = 2x + 1\), \(x = 3\) and \(y = 7\): the fraction is \(\frac{3}{7}\).

Check: \(\frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}\).

subtopic: Fractional and Negative Indices

answer: A
flawed: yes
note: As printed (a plus sign), the value is \(\sqrt{\frac{5}{16}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{4} \approx 0.56\), which is no option. With a division sign, the usual textbook form of this question, the value is \(\frac{1}{2}\), so the plus sign looks like a misprint.
notice: As printed (with a plus sign) the value is \(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{4}\), which is not an option. The key assumes the plus sign is a misprint for a division sign, which gives \(\frac{1}{2}\).
stem:
The value of \(\left[8^{-\frac{4}{3}} + 2^{-2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\) is
A: \(\frac{1}{2}\)
B: 2
C: \(\frac{1}{4}\)
D: 4
solution:
\(8^{-\frac{4}{3}} = (2^3)^{-\frac{4}{3}} = 2^{-4} = \frac{1}{16}\) and \(2^{-2} = \frac{1}{4}\).

As printed: \(\left[\frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{4}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{5}{16}} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{4} \approx 0.56\), which matches no option.

With division in place of the plus sign: \(\left[\frac{1}{16} \div \frac{1}{4}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}} = \left[\frac{1}{4}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}\), the intended answer.
